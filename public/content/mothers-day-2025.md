# Mother's Day

![Charlie](../charlie.JPG)

When I was in 6th grade, Harry was playing football at Obsidian, which meant for a whole term, the car rides home would just be me and Mom. Every day after school she’d always have two McDonald’s Diet Cokes in the car for us. It was really nice getting to chat with her just the two of us, at a time where that was honestly pretty rare with all of the kids still at home. Beginning middle school was pretty hard for me, just when I had started to adjust to living in Oregon and going to Sage, I had to get ready for a whole new school and it didn’t have a lot of my friends. I don’t really ever remember that time as hard, though. Because no matter what happened to me that day, that 10 minute car ride with Mom always meant that I was getting home with a smile on my face.

—Charlie

![Joe](../joe-1.JPG)

One birthday, Mom and Dad got me a highly coveted Nintendo DS. I had wanted it for a long time (well, I couldn't have been older than 10, so not _that_ long), and I was so excited to get it. I remember one of the games that came with it was New Super Mario Bros, and I had never heard of it. Quickly, I discovered that it was much harder than anything I had played before, and so I got frustrated and gave up. I switched to the mini-game mode instead and enjoyed that for the rest of the day.

That evening, when we went to dinner at _Madonna Inn_ (sorry that this is where I picked), Mom asked me how my new games were. I excitedly showed her the current mini-game I was working on, and she was confused. "Why aren't you playing the regular mode?" _How does she know what regular mode is?_ I was shocked (and excited) to learn that not only had she known what the game was, she had played it! And not only has she played it, but _she was good at it!_

It was fun to get to play with her (and sometimes watch her when it was too hard). I didn't know she had ever played video games before, I had only ever played with Dad, and he would throw up if it went on too long. That was the start of a lot of fun memories, including her playing the Wii with us, and her spraining(?) her wrist after playing Pirates of the Caribbean too hard. It was also the first time I remember realizing that there were things about Mom I didn't know! Before that, I figured she had been there my whole life, so I had probably been there her whole life.

—Joe

1. Once on a Saturday morning, mom and I went to the house of bread factory to use their mixer. I don’t remember what we were doing contextually other than we needed to use their large mixer. They were very clear that we couldn’t add anything to the mixer while it was running. I immediately forgot about the new rule. At one point I dumped a very large bag of flower into the running mixer. It blew the flour all over everywhere, especially our clothes. I remember being relieved that mom was laughing about it because it meant I could also laugh about it and it was a really funny experience.

2. I remember a few different times when I was ages 8-10 where mom would lay down with her kindle on the couch in the piano room and would have me come rub her feet and taught me how to give a woman a foot massage. To quote Samuel L Jackson, “I’m the foot f\*cking master, I got my technique down and everything. I don’t be tickling or nothing.” Anyways, 15 years later, my wife is grateful to have a mother-in-law that taught her son how to give a woman a foot massage.

3. One December, I was home alone and a package arrived. We were all under explicit instruction not to open any packages because probably all of them were Christmas presents. I couldn’t resist, and figured it was probably a gift for one of my siblings, so I opened it. I peaked just enough to see the LEGO logo and the blades of a helicopter on the box. My heart sank, it was the heavy duty, dual blade police helicopter, the very one I had asked for, and I had opened it prematurely. I called mom and she expressed her disappointment in my disobedience, but sadly told me that because I opened it and spoiled the gift, she would have to send it back and I wouldn’t be getting it for Christmas. The humility and gleeful relief I felt on Christmas Day to open a present and see again those same helicopter blades resonates with me still. When I had transgressed the rules of Christmas and had accepted the consequences, I was met with a forgiving mother who mercifully put legos back under the tree.

Happy Mother’s Day to the best Mom out there! Thanks you for being a Mom who taught me to laugh when the flour explodes everywhere. To love and serve the women in my life, no matter how ugly she thinks her feet are (they aren’t that bad). And for teaching me that I have parents, both earthly and Heavenly, who will mercifully put life’s gifts back under the tree, even when I have sinned. I am proud to be your son, and thrilled that one day I will have kids that will call you Grandma! I LOVE YOU MOM!!

—Harry
